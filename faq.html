01 · Client guide

The basics

01

How much does it cost?

Nothing. There are no fees at any stage. Court or government costs, like filing fees, are explained to you first. If anyone asks for payment in the clinic’s name, report it to the Clinical Program Director.

02

Who can ask for help?

Anyone can book. We put first the people who can’t afford a lawyer, such as low-income families, women and children facing abuse, senior citizens, PWDs and PDLs. Eligibility is checked at your interview, free.

03

What kind of help can I get?

Legal advice, information about your rights, and help preparing documents like affidavits and demand letters. When appropriate, we help settle disputes or represent you. If another office can help better, we refer you.

04

Will a student handle my case?

Yes, with a lawyer behind every step. Law student practitioners interview you, research and draft under Rule 138-A. A supervising lawyer reviews and signs all work before it reaches you.

05

Is what I share kept private?

Yes. Everything you tell us is confidential and protected by lawyer-client privilege and RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012). Interviews take place in a private room.

06

How do I book a visit?

Book online at clinic.snmclaw.com in English or Bisaya. It takes six short steps. Need help with the form? Email legal-clinic@snmclaw.com.

07

What should I bring?

A valid ID, your booking reference, and copies and originals of papers about your concern: contracts, receipts, text messages, barangay records or court notices. Bring a certificate of indigency if you have one.

08

What happens at my first visit?

A law student practitioner interviews you, and you sign the printed intake form in front of staff. We check eligibility and conflicts, then tell you how we can help.

09

Does booking make the clinic my lawyer?

Not yet. The clinic provides free legal advice through supervised law student practitioners under Rule 138-A. Booking does not automatically establish an attorney-client relationship.

10

Can the clinic turn down my case?

Yes. The Clinic reviews each request and reserves the right to decline a case for valid reasons. For example, we may decline if:

  • Helping you would create a conflict of interest, such as when the Clinic already assists the other party in the same dispute.
  • You already have a lawyer handling the same matter.
  • Your request falls outside the Clinic’s services or eligibility guidelines.
  • The Clinic lacks the resources or available supervising lawyers to handle your case properly or meet an urgent deadline.

If we cannot take your case, we will let you know and explain the reason when we can. Where appropriate, we will refer you to the Public Attorney’s Office (PAO), the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP), or another office or agency that may be able to assist you.

02 · Booking online

Booking online: your questions

About the Book a Visit form at clinic.snmclaw.com, from picking a slot to getting your reference.

Have these ready

A mobile number, a valid ID (type your name and birthday as they appear on it), your household’s monthly income (estimates are fine), and the name of the person or company your concern is against.

01

Do I need to qualify before I book?

No. Booking is free and open to everyone. You don’t need to prove anything first. Eligibility is checked at your intake interview.

02

Can I fill in the form in Bisaya?

Yes. At the top of Step 1, under Language / Pinulongan, choose EN for English or VS for Bisaya. The questions and buttons switch at once.

03

What does the form ask?

Six steps, one at a time: a slot, your details, your household, your legal concern, what happened, and consent. The progress bar shows where you are, and you can go back to any finished step.

04

Which days and times can I pick?

Only the highlighted consultation days, up to 90 days ahead. Slots marked Not available are taken or have passed. Pick a day, then a time.

05

Why does the form ask about my income?

Only to check if you qualify for free legal aid, following Public Attorney’s Office standards. List who lives with you and what each earns a month. Estimates are fine.

06

Why do you need the other party’s name?

The clinic can’t act against its own clients, so we check this name before anything else. If there are several, separate the names with semicolons.

07

Can I upload my documents?

You can attach photos or PDFs, but for now only their file names reach the clinic. Bring copies and the originals to your appointment.

08

Is my information safe?

Yes. It is used only to assess your request and is protected under RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012). If you don’t agree to the privacy notice, the form can’t be sent online, but you can email the clinic instead.

09

What happens after I submit?

You get a reference like SNMC-2026-4K7Q2D. Take a screenshot or photo of it and show it at reception on your appointment day. Clinic staff review your request first.

10

What if the form won’t go through?

Booking closed? It opens again at the hours shown on the page. Too many requests? Wait a few minutes, then submit again. Something went wrong? Try again. Your answers stay on the page.

03 · Getting help

Getting help from the clinic

More answers about who we help, how we help, and where we can reach you.

Not sure we can help?

Book anyway, or email us. If your concern is outside what the clinic can do, we tell you why and point you to the office that can.

01

Do I have to live in Lapu-Lapu City?

No. You can still book. We check at your intake interview whether the clinic can help, and if another office is better placed, we point you there.

02

What kinds of cases do you handle?

Criminal, civil, labor, family, land and more. You don’t need the legal term. Describe your concern in a few words, and we sort out the category with you at intake.

03

Can you represent me in court?

Student practitioners can’t appear in court on their own. If your case needs a lawyer in court, the clinic tells you how it can help, and may refer you to PAO or the IBP.

04

Do you come to our barangay or island?

The clinic holds legal missions in barangays, island communities and detention facilities from time to time. You can book online at any time.

05

I have a disability or trouble reading. Can you help?

Yes. We can arrange a Filipino Sign Language interpreter, large-print materials and an accessible room. Tell us what you need when you book.

06

I’m being hurt at home. What will you do?

We act right away. We connect you to the police Women and Children Protection Desk, the City Social Welfare and Development Office or a partner shelter, and call ahead for you.

07

I already have a PAO lawyer. Can you still help?

Usually not on the same case, because the clinic can’t take a case another lawyer already handles. You can still submit a request, and we will refer you.

08

Can someone book for me?

A relative or friend can help you fill in the form, but the details and the consent must be yours. You still come to the intake interview yourself.

09

How do I report misconduct by clinic staff?

Report it to the Clinical Program Director. The clinic has zero tolerance for abuse, harassment or asking for money. Complaints are handled in confidence.

04 · Your visit

Before and after your visit

Practical answers about booking, waiting, and keeping in touch with the clinic.

Keep your reference.

Take a screenshot or photo of it after you submit, and bring it with a valid ID to every visit. Have it ready whenever you contact the clinic.

01

Do I need an email address?

No. Only a mobile number is required. It is how the clinic reaches you about your booking, so type it carefully. Email is optional.

02

Can I book using my phone?

Yes. The form works on a phone or a computer. You need to be online with JavaScript turned on. If you see a notice about it, try another browser.

03

How soon will I hear from you?

Clinic staff review every request before your visit and contact you by mobile if anything needs to change. Your appointment stands unless we tell you otherwise.

04

I have a court date coming up. What do I do?

Put the date and the court in Step 5. If the hearing is within 48 hours, don’t wait for your slot. Go to the nearest PAO or IBP office now.

05

Why is booking open only at certain hours?

To keep the clinic’s bookings safe from spam and abuse. Outside those hours, the page tells you when it opens again. You can also email the clinic.

06

What is the security check?

A tick box that may show above Submit request. It keeps automated spam out. Tick it, then submit.

07

Can I cancel or move my appointment?

Yes. Email the clinic as early as you can, with your name and reference number. Staff will help you pick a new slot.

08

What if I lose my reference or miss my visit?

Email the clinic with your full name and mobile number. Staff can find your request and help you book again.

09

Will you keep me updated on my case?

Yes. Once the clinic takes your case, your student practitioner updates you at least every 15 days, and within a day of any notice from a court or office.

10

Can you help with travel, medical or document costs?

If you qualify as indigent, you may get help with travel to court, required medical exams and document fees. Bring a barangay certificate of indigency and ask at your interview.
